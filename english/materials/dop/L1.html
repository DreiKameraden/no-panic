Present Tenses in IT & Aviation

How software engineers and aircraft technicians use Present Simple, Continuous, and Perfect in their daily work

🔥 Warm-up: Read and Think

Read the text below. Pay attention to the highlighted words — you'll need them later.

When Code Meets the Sky

Alex works as a software engineer at a company that develops diagnostic systems for aircraft. Every morning, he reviews bug reports from the previous day and prioritizes tasks for his team. Currently, he is working on a new algorithm that detects malfunctions in the turbine system.

"The framework we use handles real-time data from sensors installed on the fuselage," Alex explains. "Right now, we are testing a new module that analyzes vibration patterns. If the system identifies an anomaly, it triggers an alert for the maintenance crew."

His colleague, Maria, is an aircraft technician. She inspects the landing gear and hydraulic systems every day. "I have been working in aviation for five years," she says. "Recently, we have implemented Alex's software, and it has significantly reduced our downtime. Last week, the system detected a crack in the wing structure that we had missed during a routine inspection."

"The integration of software and hardware has revolutionized the industry," Alex adds. "We have already deployed three updates this year, and we are planning to enhance the predictive capabilities of the system."

📚 Grammar: Present Simple

Use: Habits, routines, general truths, permanent situations

Structure: Subject + verb (s/es for he/she/it)

Signal words: every day, usually, always, often, sometimes, never

✅ Alex works as a software engineer. (permanent job)

✅ He reviews bug reports every morning. (routine)

✅ The framework handles real-time data. (general truth)

📚 Grammar: Present Continuous

Use: Actions happening now, temporary situations, current projects

Structure: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing

Signal words: now, right now, currently, at the moment, this week

✅ He is working on a new algorithm. (current project)

✅ Right now, we are testing a new module. (happening now)

✅ We are planning to enhance the system. (current intention)

📚 Grammar: Present Perfect

Use: Past actions with present results, experiences, changes over time, completed actions with present relevance

Structure: Subject + have/has + past participle (V3)

Signal words: already, yet, just, recently, ever, never, so far, this year

✅ Maria has been working in aviation for five years. (started in past, continues now)

✅ We have implemented Alex's software. (past action, present result)

✅ It has significantly reduced our downtime. (change over time)

✅ We have already deployed three updates this year. (completed, present relevance)
